MCAS, EDS and POTS: why they often appear together

Hypermobile EDS, POTS and MCAS are three separate conditions, but for many people they come as a set. Here's what's known about why, how they affect each other, and how to make sense of them day to day.

Why they often turn up together

Doctors and patient groups have noticed for years that hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome or hypermobility spectrum disorder (hEDS or HSD), postural tachycardia syndrome (POTS) and mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS) seem to appear together more often than chance would suggest. People who live with all three often call it the trifecta.

The reasons aren't fully understood yet, and research is ongoing. Some of the ideas being explored are:

  • Stretchier connective tissue: if blood vessel walls are more stretchy, more blood may pool in the legs on standing, which could contribute to POTS
  • Mast cell chemicals: histamine and other chemicals released by mast cells can widen blood vessels and speed up the heart, which can make POTS symptoms worse
  • Shared triggers: heat, exertion, illness, poor sleep and hormonal changes can set off all three

Not everyone with one of these conditions has the others. But if you have one, it's worth knowing about the others and mentioning any symptoms to your doctor.

Where the symptoms overlap

Part of what makes the trifecta confusing is that many symptoms could come from any of the three:

SymptomCould be linked to
Fatigue and brain fogAll three
Dizziness and a fast heartbeatPOTS and MCAS
Nausea, bloating and gut painAll three
HeadachesAll three
Joint pain, subluxations and dislocationsEDS and HSD
Flushing, hives and itchingMCAS

That overlap makes it hard to tell what's driving a bad day. Is the dizziness POTS, or the start of a mast cell reaction? Is the fatigue from a joint flare, or from standing too long?

Different conditions, different timing

One of the most useful things to know is that the three often react on different timescales:

  • POTS symptoms tend to show up the same day, often within minutes or hours of standing, heat or a big meal
  • MCAS reactions can build up over a day or two, as several triggers add together
  • EDS and HSD often bring payback the next day, after a busy or physical day

So the trigger for today's bad day might have been yesterday, or the day before. If you only look at what happened on the day, you can easily blame the wrong thing.

Tracking all three together

Keeping separate notes for each condition hides the links between them. Tracking them side by side, in one log, lets you see:

  • Which days more than one condition flared at once
  • Which triggers set off more than one condition
  • Whether a busy day leads to a mast cell reaction that evening and joint pain the next morning
  • Whether a change in medicine, salt, fluids or physio is actually helping

It also makes appointments easier. Many people with the trifecta see several specialists who each focus on one condition. A single summary that covers all three helps each of them see the whole picture.

Tips for appointments

  • Bring a one page summary rather than months of notes
  • Note your three or four biggest problems and what you'd like help with
  • Mention all three conditions to every specialist, along with your medicines
  • Ask how a new treatment for one condition might affect the others. For example, some painkillers used for joint pain can trigger mast cell reactions

Common questions

What is the trifecta?

It's an informal name used by patients for having hypermobile EDS or HSD, POTS and MCAS together. It isn't a medical diagnosis in itself.

If I have hEDS, will I get POTS and MCAS too?

Not necessarily. Many people with hEDS or HSD don't have POTS or MCAS. But they're reported together often enough that it's worth mentioning any symptoms that might fit to your doctor.

What's the best way to track MCAS, EDS and POTS?

Use one daily log that covers all three, rate symptoms the same way each day, note triggers and what you did, and look back over two or three days rather than just the day of a flare. TrinityMate was made to do exactly that.
